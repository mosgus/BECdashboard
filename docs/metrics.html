📊 Key Metrics Glossary

Quick-Reference Table — Definitions, Interpretation, and Benchmarks

Metric Plain-English Definition Interpretation
📈 Return Metrics
CAGR Compound Annual Growth Rate. Average annual return, smoothed over the period. > 10%: Excellent
5–10%: Good
< 0%: Underwater
Total Return Price appreciation + dividends reinvested, as a percentage. Compare to benchmark. Benchmark is target.
Arithmetic Return Simple average of period returns. Sensitive to volatility. Used in some risk models. Usually > CAGR if volatile.
⚠️ Risk Metrics
Volatility Standard deviation of returns. Higher = wilder price swings. 10–15%: Conservative
15–25%: Moderate
> 25%: Aggressive
Beta Sensitivity to market moves. Beta=1 means tracks market; >1 means amplifies. 0.8–1.2: Market-tracking
< 0.8: Lower market risk
> 1.5: High volatility
Max Drawdown Largest peak-to-trough loss during the period, as a percentage. > −10%: Resilient
−10% to −30%: Normal
< −30%: Severe stress
VaR (95%) Worst 5% loss in a typical day/week/month. "We are 95% confident losses won't exceed this." Daily VaR > 2% = high daily risk. Monthly VaR for stress-test baseline.
CVaR (95%) Average loss on the worst 5% days. Tail risk—what happens in the 5% crash scenarios. Always worse than VaR. Monitor quarterly. Flag if > risk limit.
⚖️ Risk-Adjusted Return Metrics
Sharpe Ratio Return per unit of risk. (Return − Risk-Free Rate) / Volatility. Higher is better. > 1.0: Excellent
0.5–1.0: Good
< 0.5: Weak risk-adjusted return
Sortino Ratio Like Sharpe, but penalizes only downside volatility. Rewards strategies that avoid crashes. Usually > Sharpe. > 1.5: Strong downside protection.
Calmar Ratio CAGR / Max Drawdown. Measures return per unit of maximum pain. > 1.0: Good. Emphasizes drawdown recovery.
Information Ratio Excess return vs. benchmark, per unit of tracking error. (Your Return − Benchmark Return) / Tracking Error. > 0.5: Good alpha. < 0.1: No edge over benchmark.
🏗️ Portfolio Composition Metrics
HHI (Herfindahl) Concentration index. HHI = Σ(weighti)2. Range: 0 (equal weight) to 1 (single stock). < 0.10: Well-diversified
0.10–0.20: Moderate
> 0.20: Concentrated risk
MCTR (Marginal Contribution to Risk) How much a 1% increase in position weight increases portfolio volatility. Risk per marginal dollar. High MCTR positions = high risk contributors. Use to rebalance toward lower-MCTR assets.
Correlation −1 to +1. How closely two assets move together. +1 = perfect sync; −1 = opposite; 0 = independent. Low correlation = diversification benefit. Avoid portfolio of high-correlation pairs.
📊 Factor & Style Exposures (Fama-French)
Market (MKT) Equity risk premium. Beta-adjusted market return. Usually positive. Negative = defensive positioning.
Size (SMB) Small Minus Big. Exposure to small-cap premium. Positive = tilted to small caps. Negative = large cap.
Value (HML) High Minus Low. Exposure to value stocks (high book-to-market). Positive = value tilt. Negative = growth tilt.
Momentum Exposure to recent winners vs. losers. Positive = follow recent strength. Negative = contrarian.
💰 CAPM & Optimization Metrics
Expected Return CAPM expected return. rf + β(MRP), where MRP = market risk premium. Forward-looking. Used in CAPM optimizer to weight positions.
Conviction Your view of mis-pricing. "% Undervalued" in CAPM. Adjusts expected return. +50% conviction = you think it's worth 50% more. Feeds optimizer.
Optimal Weight Position size recommended by CAPM optimizer, given your convictions. Compare to current weight. Gap = suggested trade.
🎲 Monte Carlo Metrics
Terminal Stats End-of-horizon distribution: median, p5, p95, probability of loss. P5 = downside scenario. P95 = upside. "Prob Loss" = risk of ending below start.
Brier Score Calibration metric. 0 = perfect. Measures whether your predicted range was accurate. "Well-calibrated" = your forecasts are reliable. "Overconfident" = range too narrow.
💡 Pro Tip: Sharpe and Sortino ratios are risk-adjusted return metrics—they let you compare "apples to apples" between conservative and aggressive portfolios. Higher is always better.
⚠️ Watch Out: VaR and CVaR are based on historical distributions. In tail events (market crashes), actual losses can exceed the modeled VaR. These are guidelines, not guarantees.
